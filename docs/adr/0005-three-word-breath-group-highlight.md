The sentence stays the tracking unit (ADR-0003), but the overlay paints a three-word breath group instead of the whole sentence: the group under the voice in amber, the next three words faintly, and a dotted rule plus a deeper wash on the exact word being spoken. A three-word group is about one breath, so the reader always sees the few words they are reading now and where they go next, without a full paragraph of ink competing for attention. Word alignment is display-only: the recent spoken tail is matched against the current sentence's words to choose the group, and it may move the dotted marker on a single word, but it can never move the sentence cursor — relocation still needs the multi-word evidence ADR-0003 requires. Word rectangles come from Vision's per-substring bounding boxes, captured at the same OCR pass as the line boxes; when a line has no word geometry the overlay falls back to the whole-sentence rectangles. Rejected: per-word karaoke wash (constant motion, and it needs word-level tracking to stay honest), and a fixed highlight on the entire sentence (too much ink; the eyes lose the exact line being read).
