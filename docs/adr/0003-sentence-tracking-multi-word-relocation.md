# The sentence is the tracking unit, and only multi-word evidence moves the cursor

Tracking works on sentences, not screen lines or words: the sentence under the cursor owns the strong highlight and the next sentence a weaker cue, and the cursor never moves on a single-word match. A run of spoken words that matches another sentence — the next one, a later one, or one already read — relocates the cursor there, forward or backward. This rejects word-level karaoke and naive every-match tracking, both of which let one stray word or one speech-recognition error pull the highlight off the reader's actual place. An earlier attempt failed exactly there: one or two words matching a distant line yanked the highlight away. The multi-word threshold and nearest-sentence tie-break exist to make that impossible; the alignment scoring is expected to keep evolving. The overlay's word marker (ADR-0005) is display-only and never feeds back into cursor movement.
